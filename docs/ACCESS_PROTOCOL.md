# Access changes and key epochs

Album-only publication has an independent identity and media key. `invite_album` creates a fresh encrypted snapshot of the current selection and metadata for the new viewer; parent keys, prior audience history, deleted photos and old captions are excluded. New grants on an existing album scope are refused after its initial publication journal changes. Previously authorized viewers retain their historical scope, and `publish_album` updates every established audience explicitly. Old downloaded copies remain readable to their existing recipients. Audience setup, native management parity and deployed gateway validation remain release work.

An ordinary invitation now contains an owner-signed, recipient-sealed envelope. Its Room secret and grant are encrypted for the intended device before the token leaves the core. Legacy plaintext version-one invitations remain readable for migration.

A Room retains a stable original identity key and its historical media keys. Device removal creates a fresh key epoch for new originals and catalog packets. The owner signs a chained access revision containing the active grants, hashes of previously accepted operations, and sealed key bundles for remaining devices. New invitees receive the historical keys needed to browse existing memories.

Recipients verify the Room and owner, signatures, revision sequence, previous-record hash, canonical device keys and committed operation hashes. Conflicting policy branches are rejected. Older valid prefixes cannot downgrade a device. A removed author cannot replace a committed operation by reusing its ID: the owner's committed hash must still match. Pending edits invalidated by an access change remain in the encrypted local catalog for recovery rather than entering the shared projection.

Each original records its encryption epoch independently of its asset ID. Old originals keep their keys when the Room changes membership; re-importing an evicted copy preserves both its identity and epoch. Catalog packet version two carries the signed access chain and an encrypted journal. Older packets can merge through the current permission checks without reverting access.

The gateway persists signed policy chains and enforces current device membership, role and access revision on media/catalog requests. A removed device may retrieve its own removal policy, but receives no new key envelope. Only the owner may update access. Provider credentials remain local to the owning device or gateway. Ordinary storage adapters carry the same signed, recipient-sealed policy as an additional object.

Revocation is effective at a gateway after its update is acknowledged and on devices after they learn the signed revision. An offline device cannot know about an unseen removal; data it uploaded under an old key before reconnecting may still be readable under that old key. Previously downloaded content cannot be remotely erased. Providers withholding updates cannot downgrade a device that has already observed them; no claim of universal instantaneous offline revocation is made.

The device-sealing implementation uses RustCrypto's libsodium-compatible sealed boxes and the documented Ed25519-to-X25519 conversion for existing device identities. Ephemeral sender keys are generated per envelope; the owner signature authenticates the envelope. This preserves existing device codes and recoverable identities. Separate encryption identities are recommended for a later protocol migration, and independent cryptographic review remains a release gate. See [Libsodium conversion guidance](https://doc.libsodium.org/advanced/ed25519-curve25519) and [RustCrypto sealed boxes](https://docs.rs/crypto_box/0.9.1/crypto_box/struct.PublicKey.html).

Preserved offline work can be exported explicitly using `export_preserved`. Its unencrypted ZIP contains signed quarantined operations, private project versions, verified locally available originals (including linked components), and a manifest identifying unavailable files. The Apple and Android interfaces explain the plaintext export before saving it. Exporting neither republishes rejected changes nor restores membership. Full encrypted recovery backups also preserve quarantined originals.

Current limits include 256 active devices per Room, 4,096 signed access revisions, and a 32 MiB storage index/policy limit. Compact policy checkpoints and catalog paging remain work for large libraries. Ownership transfer, member profiles spanning multiple devices, native reintegration of preserved edits, and all-platform recovery interfaces remain incomplete.
