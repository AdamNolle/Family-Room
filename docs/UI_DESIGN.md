# Cozy Glass — design contract

Family Room is a repeated-use native app. Adam's **photographic instrument** direction supplies its composition: photography dominates, with compact metadata and recognizable selection. Remy's gallery continuity informs grid/detail relationships; Raycast's precision informs controls and the editing workspace. Their logos, entrance gates, red atmosphere, and promotional layouts are not copied.

- **Type:** native serif for Room names and welcome headings; native sans for reading and actions; monospaced digits for dates, transfers, and timecodes. Native text scaling remains available.
- **Composition:** one real photographic anchor on Home, followed by actual Moments and recent contributions. A regular contact sheet is the default library; the scattered wall represents the same assets. Phones use a single column and native tabs. Desktops/tablets use a quiet sidebar and larger canvas.
- **Surfaces:** warm off-white, neutral photo beds, dark ink, terracotta actions. Dark mode uses neutral charcoal. Glass is reserved for chrome and contextual panels; reduced transparency uses solid fills. Wood, fabric, grain, and lighting are not stacked over working screens.
- **Assets and icons:** actual imported media in populated image slots, truthful availability placeholders otherwise, native utility symbols. Reference captures are research, not production assets. No invented members, photos, or storage metrics enter the real library.
- **Interaction:** Search and Add remain visible. Person filters expose any/all and clear controls. Selection retains the Room; Room changes clear filters. Details show media before caption, date, tags, and notes; diagnostics expand separately.
- **Editor:** separate preview, media, timeline, and inspector regions, clear selected layers, stable time marks. Drafts remain private until publication. Progress and error states stay visible.
- **States:** create/join/recovery, empty, loading, missing originals, waiting transfers, validation errors, and completed exports are designed alongside populated screens. Decorative motion never blocks an action; reduced motion keeps the same controls.

Android applies the same warm canvas and type roles through Material 3. Home and each browsing destination have focused content; Room creation, membership and storage live in settings. Library uses an adaptive contact sheet. Search and Add remain available, and activity restoration preserves navigation. Utility glyphs use a consistent 24-unit grid, 1.8-unit rounded outline and native tint; labels identify every destination and ambiguous action. Apple primary actions keep their label and fill together, so inactive macOS windows retain readable contrast. Empty libraries and empty filtered results have distinct explanations; changing Rooms clears all filters.

Source review and palette measurements are complete: secondary text has 5.20:1 contrast on the light canvas and 9.36:1 on the dark canvas; white text on the primary action has 6.52:1. Native builds and actual movie tests pass. Fresh/returning iPhone flows and an Android native onboarding/navigation/settings/search/restoration test pass. ### Motion and icon language

Theater gives a **published** film one short physical entrance: its actual cover frame appears on a black-and-white rimmed disc, lifts and tilts, then slides into a quiet player deck before the native movie starts. The transition belongs to opening a film, not to browsing every memory. An original must be locally available or successfully fetched and verified before playback; a waiting original gets a retry state. Closing the screening stops playback; Android clears its temporary file, while Apple reuses the app's viewing cache. Apple keeps one SwiftUI screening surface with native AVKit playback; on macOS a narrow AppKit `AVPlayerView` bridge avoids a SwiftUI AVKit crash observed on macOS 27. The DVD motif remains confined to Theater; this is a viewing gesture, not physical DVD authoring.

Photographs and videos keep their real colors. On macOS, gallery cards gain a very small hover lift and tilt; Apple and Android cards spring back after a press. The motion keeps the black-and-white photo outline intact and never runs on an idle loop. Navigation and empty-state symbols use one rounded icon tile family with pale-blue neutral tiles and brown selected tiles. The selected destination icon makes a small spring lift; utility icons retain text labels. System reduced-motion settings bypass the disc sequence, photo transforms and icon lift while preserving the same controls and direct playback. No sound or haptic fires automatically.

## Native gallery refinement

The user's 2026-10-02 direction uses [Nolle Studios](https://nollestudios.com/) as a material reference, then explicitly narrows that inspiration: liquid glass and photo outlines in black and white, with no cork background and no clone of the portfolio. Family Room keeps its own navigation and Room-centered composition.

Home places the Room header above a featured memory and a separate horizontal memory strip. Library keeps its adaptive grid and optional scattered wall. Photography retains its original colors, surrounded by a white mat, black inner keyline and black outer edge. The user's final palette direction explicitly rejects green. Warm white (#F7F5F1), taupe (#F0EAE3), white and charcoal form the surfaces; orange-brown (#8A4D32) provides readable action controls, muted apricot (#E8C1A4) supplies small decorative details, and pale blue (#E2EBF1) supports glass tint and selected secondary surfaces. Dark mode uses warm charcoal and brown, with pale orange action ink. Photo frames remain strictly black and white, and photography is never graded by the palette. No cork texture is shipped.

Apple uses native Liquid Glass on iOS/macOS 26+, regular material on earlier versions and solid surfaces with reduced transparency. Android uses native Compose controls and a translucent tonal surface; it does not claim Apple's Liquid Glass API. Instrument Serif supplies selected display headings under its bundled SIL Open Font License; scalable system fonts handle reading and controls. No moving background texture is used.

Live visual review uses an isolated local development Room with public portfolio photos imported through the native file picker. These photos are not bundled sample library content or shared with other members/providers. Screenshots and native build checks document the reviewed state; they do not certify cross-platform sharing, provider connectivity or complete accessibility.

### Refinement across destinations

Albums now present real cover photos in an adaptive native grid, with a compact, expandable creation control. People use named, accessible monograms with confirmed-tag counts; they do not fabricate recognized faces or imply membership. Both screens retain actual catalog commands and native navigation. Home does not repeat its highlighted media in the later recent grid. Phone highlights are shorter than desktop/tablet highlights. Library uses one vertical scroll container with native filters and search, preventing nested layout measurement from pushing the header outside the desktop window. Photo previews are bounded to their cells before cropping; original aspect and data are preserved in the detail/export paths.

An album can carry a short note and a chosen photograph as its cover. The photograph leads the card and detail view inside the monochrome photo outline; the note stays secondary. Cover selection lives beside the album's ordered memories, so changing it never implies deleting or moving the original. An unavailable cover falls back to another visible memory.

### Room ownership controls
Ownership succession stays in Room settings beside the member roster. The current owner chooses an existing device and shares its signed offer privately. The recipient pastes the offer and confirms a plain-language review describing the new role, key rotation, and the limit on recalling downloaded copies. Success feedback distinguishes a local transfer from an update published to connected storage. Use platform-native sharing and confirmation surfaces; do not add ceremonial screens or hide the action behind a gesture.
